What is Lapis Lazuli and its Ancient Roots?

Lapis Lazuli is a striking blue metamorphic rock. It has been prized for thousands of years. Its rich color comes from the mineral lazurite. Small flecks of golden pyrite often shimmer within its deep blue.

Ancient civilizations treasured Lapis Lazuli. Egyptians used it in burial masks, scarabs, and jewelry. They believed it brought protection and divine connection. Cleopatra herself reportedly used powdered Lapis Lazuli as eyeshadow.

The Sumerians and Akkadians associated Lapis with royalty and the heavens. They thought it contained the souls of the gods. This belief highlights its deep spiritual significance from early human history.

Its name means 'blue stone' from the Latin 'lapis' for stone and Persian 'lazward' for blue. This simple naming reflects its most prominent feature. Its history is tied to art, power, and spiritual practices.

Archaeological finds in Afghanistan show Lapis mines active since 7000 BCE. This makes it one of the oldest known gemstone materials. Its enduring appeal crosses cultures and millennia, linking us to ancient wisdom.
